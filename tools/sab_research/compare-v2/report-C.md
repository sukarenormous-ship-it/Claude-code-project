### How a tool section is built
Scenario in small integers → compute → name → formula + "อ่านสมการ" → vary one thing → what this does *not* yet tell you → PX hand-off. Example: T2.2 (`ex:2979–3009`): 10 vs 4, keep 80% of the gap → 8.8; names AR(1); formula; then ε = +2 gives 10.8 "ซึ่งสูงกว่าวันนี้ได้" (`ex:2989`); φ table with 0.80 / 0.98 / 1.00 / 1.02 / −0.50; figure.

### How a case is built (question → data → holdings → ledger → conclusion: yes, consistently)
K0x.1 the question and the source; K0x.2 data → weights → **value ÷ price = units** → P&L per leg → fees on every trade → net → comparison table; K0x.3 run command, what was shown vs what still has to be proved, what real data is needed, PX hand-off. Trace for K01: question `ex:3485` → data `ex:3497–3503` → weights `ex:3507–3515` → units `ex:3517` → P&L `ex:3519` → fees/net `ex:3521` → three-row comparison `ex:3523–3527` → limits `ex:3537–3541` → real-data needs + PX7 `ex:3543`.

## 2. Topic-by-topic comparison

### 2.1 Funding: displayed rate → cash received
- **Example (K05, `ex:3710–3798`)**: two perps on venues L and H; per-event cash = base × rate with the base *re-read each event* ("ฐานของแต่ละรอบใช้ราคาและจำนวนที่มีสิทธิ์รับหรือจ่ายในรอบนั้น จึงไม่ได้ตรึงอยู่ที่มูลค่าตอนเปิดสถานะ", `ex:3728`); sign flips in round 2; three-part reconciliation −0.5 + (−0.01095) − 0.4075 = −0.91845 (`ex:3747`); eligibility ("การวางคำสั่งรอไว้ยังไม่ทำให้มีสิทธิ์รับ funding ตามจำนวนเป้าหมาย", `ex:3753`); base price is venue-defined (oracle vs mark, `ex:3718`); funding ≠ borrow (`ex:3751`); split collateral 50 / 10 against a floor of 15 (`ex:3779–3788`).
- **Ours (ch 7)**: spot + short perp; three-round table; ladder 10.95 → 8.76 → 7.98 → 3.98; four 90-round paths (−80…+1,120); exit rule that helps +420 then hurts −370; crowding 10 → 5 → 3; accident table; break-even "8 รอบ".
- **Verdict**: ours is clearer and far more useful on *"is it worth doing"* (capital denominator, hurdle, path dependence, exit rule) — the example never annualises, never divides by capital, never tests a rule. The example is clearer on *the mechanical ledger*: who pays on each leg, which price is the base, when you are entitled, and that money in another venue cannot cover margin here. Despite its title the example's case does not actually get from "อัตราที่เห็น" to an annual figure; ours does. **Gaps in ours**: no explicit sign convention; no statement that the base is the venue's price at the funding timestamp (we say only "Mark Price × จำนวน × อัตรา"); no eligibility rule; no perp-vs-perp two-venue version (the form most retail "funding arb" bots use); no split-collateral table.

### 2.4 Sizing under capital limits
- **Example (T4, K08, PX4, PX14)**: two opportunities, two bottlenecks, enumerate integer pairs (`ex:3232–3246`); rounding down picks the wrong portfolio (`ex:3254`); mean–variance score with λ (`ex:3270–3301`); netting baskets (`ex:3307–3323`); score → expected return → size with a quadratic penalty and "a = 0 is optimal when cost eats the forecast" (`ex:4030`).

- **Verdict**: ours is clearer on the beginner's real question — *how much can this one trade lose, therefore how big* — and has capacity and exchange ceilings the example lacks. The example is clearer on *allocating one pot across competing uses* and on rounding. We never pose "I have 1,250 and two jobs; how much of each"; ch 17's fold only asserts "คิดขนาดเหมาะสมทีละใบแล้วใส่พร้อมกันจะเกินเสมอ" with no numbers. ### 2.5 Mean-reversion forecasting
- **Example (T2, `ex:2947–3061`)**: forecast of the H-day change with a non-zero reference level; one concrete shock (expected 8.8, actual 10.8); φ table including 1.00 and 1.02; OU with κ and units; half-life read off the table before the log; forecast → trade bridge (expected −0.5, cost 0.2, net 0.3, `ex:2971`); compare against a naive baseline (`ex:2969`); "failing mean reversion is not evidence for momentum" (`ex:3057`).

- **Verdict**: ours is better on intuition and on what matters for money (paths, hitting the budget first, same rule different worlds) — the example has no path simulation at all in this portion, only expected lines. The example is better on four small steps we skip: subtract the mean before multiplying (ours fixes Mean = 0 throughout ch 13), one explicit shock, the φ ≥ 1 rows, and the baseline comparison.

## 5. Weaknesses of the example we should not copy

Arithmetic: I recomputed every worked figure in T1–T6, K01–K09 and AX1–AX15 (multiple-testing 99.4%; κ 0.2231; half-lives 3.1063 and 34.31; PCA weights and both scaling variants; Kalman 9.6 / 8.4; h-decomposition −4.05; both integer optima and the LP 10.5; variance 0.0003 and score 0.0048; K03 z 2.6295 / 1.4033; K04 2.0077 / 1.7980; K05 −0.91845 and PX11 −0.87685; box 4.0965%, −0.02205, +0.02677; loop 1,016.943 / 977.063 / 997.003; K07 3.7033 / −1.5492 / 4.7172; K08 table and the completed square; K09 ledger 997.58625; PX13 general solution; PX14 integer argument). **No arithmetic errors found.** The weaknesses are of design:

1. **Symbol collisions, admitted in the text.** `a` is the price-line intercept (`ex:2881`), then the forecast intercept ("ไม่ใช่ a ที่ใช้ตั้งเส้นราคา", `ex:2957`), then residual weights ("เป็นคนละความหมายกับ a ค่าคงที่ของสมการราคา", `ex:3144`), then fraction of capital (`ex:4002`). `beta` has two meanings (`ex:3091`). `K` is Kalman gain (`ex:3182`) and strike (`ex:3810`). `x` is spread (`ex:2960`), number of baskets (`ex:3228`) and the argument of (x)⁺ (`ex:3822`). `S` is a holdings matrix (`ex:3316`) and an asset price (`ex:3827`); `M` is the market asset (`ex:3073`) and a matrix (`ex:3135`); `C` is a copula, a cost function, an asset and a call price; `u` is a percentile and a basket vector; `H` is a horizon and a venue. Our one-letter discipline (h, β, φ, θ + appendix G clash table) is better.
2. **Too advanced for a non-quant, and it knows it.** Weak stationarity, I(1), unit root, Engle–Granger in three paragraphs (`ex:2903–2915`); "w = D inverse transpose M transpose a" (`ex:3165`); partial derivative of a copula (`ex:3344`); "sigma จะมีหน่วย U ต่อรากที่สองของวัน" (`ex:3035`); `min c transpose x` (`ex:3248`).
3. **Hand-wavy where it matters.** The ADF/KPSS p-values are invented and "ไม่ใช่ผลที่คำนวณจากราคาสามแถวข้างต้น" (`ex:2917`) — the reader cannot produce one. K01 fixes the "PCA direction" as the plain average and picks the exit prices itself ("เราเป็นผู้กำหนดราคาปลายทางเอง", `ex:3539`). Copula ends with "ยังไม่ได้บอกว่า A จะลง" (`ex:3365`) after 37 lines.
4. **K09 teaches a false comfort.** At S +5 the call is set to +3 so that the hedge "ชดเชยกันพอดี" (`ex:4101`). With delta moving 0.6 → 0.75 over that move, delta plus gamma alone would give ≈ +3.375; the exact offset needs an unstated −0.375 of decay. The same case's own Greek sub-example (`ex:4069`) shows gamma adding to the move. And the final −2.25 is explained only as "ราคา call ปลายทางและจำนวน hedge ระหว่างทางต่างจากตอนเริ่ม" (`ex:4115`) — the reader is never told it is +0.75 from re-hedging and −3 from the option losing value.
5. **Sign flips between text and exercise.** T3.5 and K02 hold short A / long B and *buy* 0.05 B (`ex:3197, 3582`); PX3 holds A +1 / B −2 and *sells* 0.05 (`ex:4566, 4808`). Deliberate (hint `ex:4694`), but a trap for beginners.

Beta 1.2 / 0.8 with +40 appears in T3.1, T3.2, K07 and PX13. Spiral repetition is fine; the same digits four times is padding.
7. **Zero-rate, frictionless set-ups that produce "free money".** K06.2 borrows 100 at 0% and ends with +1.3 for certain (`ex:3848–3859`); K06.4 turns 1,000 into 1,016.94 on stated quotes. Each is followed by a caveat, but a beginner remembers the 1.3.

9. **Walls of prose.** Median paragraph 347 characters, maximum 835; no bullets, three figures in 1,291 lines, no visual hierarchy beyond headings. Our short blocks and charts are easier to enter.
10. **Name-dropping instead of teaching.** 44 source tags in this portion (QuantConnect, ArbitrageLab, Cvxportfolio, Hummingbot, Hyperliquid…) alongside "ไม่ได้ทำซ้ำผลกำไรจากตลาดทั้งหมดของผู้เขียนต้นทาง" (`ex:3491`).

13. **Abstract units and letters** — "U", A, B, M, P, Q, L, H, X, Y. Nothing to hold on to; our BTC / perp / มิน ÷ 100 convention is better.
